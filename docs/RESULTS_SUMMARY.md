# Results Summary (frozen evidence, 2026-10-08)

All numbers below are generated by `scripts/final_evidence_tables.py` into `results/final_evidence/EVIDENCE_TABLES.md`, which is the authoritative source. They are rounded here. The method is in `docs/METHODOLOGY_FINAL.md`; defects and changes are in `docs/AUDIT_ERRATA.md`. The pre-audit version of this file is kept in `docs/superseded_2026-10-08/` and must not be cited.

**Protocol in brief.**
- Tie-preserving quintile scoring, fitted on training customers.
- Rolling origins with a 91-day holdout. Dunnhumby: 4 origins, about 2,500 households each (repurchase 92.2–93.2%). Online Retail II: 5 origins, 3,377–5,281 customers (repurchase 30.6–57.7%).
- Stratified 5-fold CV within each origin, with every transformation fit on the training folds.
- Paired customer-level bootstrap; Holm correction within each dataset.
- The Online Retail II original protocol (2010-12-09 cutoff, 365-day holdout, 4,312 customers) is reported separately.

**Predictive and structural evidence are reported separately and are not combined into one performance claim.**

## A. Fuzzy RFM-FCA vs crisp RFM-FCA (base-paper method): H1

| Dataset | Metric | Δ (fuzzy − crisp) | 95% CI | p (Holm) | Origins favourable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Online Retail II | ROC AUC | +0.0056 | [+0.0036, +0.0075] | 0.014 | 5/5 |
| Online Retail II | Spend R² | +0.0139 | [+0.0111, +0.0165] | 0.014 | 5/5 |
| Online Retail II | Invoice R² | +0.0273 | [+0.0232, +0.0316] | 0.014 | 5/5 |
| Dunnhumby | ROC AUC | +0.0210 | [+0.0152, +0.0271] | 0.014 | 4/4 |
| Dunnhumby | Spend R² | +0.0270 | [+0.0217, +0.0324] | 0.014 | 4/4 |
| Dunnhumby | Invoice R² | +0.0213 | [+0.0174, +0.0255] | 0.014 | 4/4 |

Under the rolling-origin evaluation, the pooled improvement is **statistically significant on every metric in both datasets** (Holm p = 0.014). Descriptively, it is also positive at every individual origin; the origin counts summarise consistency and are not a significance test. On the Retail II original protocol, a single holdout reported descriptively, the differences are +0.023 AUC, +0.012 Spend R² and +0.020 Invoice R². The gains are modest; on Retail II, ROC AUC improves by only 0.006.

- **Fuzzy RFM-FCA does not establish superiority over the strong spline-on-log-RFM baseline.** Five of six comparisons were not significantly different, but equivalence was not formally established (no equivalence margin was pre-specified), and Retail II Invoice R² was significantly worse by 0.032.
- The large gains over standardized raw RFM reflect that baseline's misspecification (skewed R/F/M in a linear model). A 3-feature log transform recovers most of them.
- Concepts add little over the fuzzy band memberships on Dunnhumby, but help on Retail II. The improvement over crisp RFM-FCA should therefore not be attributed to the concept structure alone.

## C. Structural stability: H3

Primary measure: core-profile Jaccard, i.e. the overlap of a customer's set of concepts with membership ≥ 0.5 between two fits. Source: `results/segment_stability/` and its `matched_count_diagnostic/` subfolder.

| Study | Dataset | Fuzzy − crisp | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Refit on 80% subsamples vs full fit | Dunnhumby | −0.005 [−0.020, +0.004] | no clear difference |
| A. Refit on 80% subsamples vs full fit | Online Retail II | +0.005 [−0.002, +0.016] | no clear difference |
| B. Quarterly re-segmentation, all customers | Dunnhumby | −0.035 [−0.040, −0.029], 0/3 pairs | **fuzzy less stable** |
| B. Quarterly re-segmentation, all customers | Online Retail II | −0.020 [−0.024, −0.017], 0/4 pairs | **fuzzy less stable** |
| B. Unchanged-behaviour subset | Dunnhumby | −0.002 [−0.018, +0.015] | no significant difference |
| B. Unchanged-behaviour subset | Online Retail II | +0.020 [+0.013, +0.029], 4/4 pairs | fuzzy more stable |

Absolute levels:
- Refit stability is about 0.95–0.97 for both crisp and fuzzy.
- Quarterly re-segmentation leaves about 0.45–0.53 of a customer's profile unchanged under every arm. Roughly half of each profile changes from one quarter to the next.

**Matched-concept-count diagnostic** (one rule, fixed in `AUDIT_ERRATA.md` §6.1 before it was run): fuzzy is truncated, per fit, to the crisp concept count by the suppressor's own quality order.

| Study | Dataset | Fuzzy at matched count − crisp |
|---|---|---|
| B. All customers (verdict target) | Dunnhumby | +0.003 [−0.002, +0.008]: gap no longer significant (pre-specified label: "explained by count") |
| B. All customers (verdict target) | Online Retail II | +0.003 [−0.001, +0.006]: gap no longer significant (pre-specified label: "explained by count") |
| A. Refit | Dunnhumby | −0.009 [−0.022, −0.002] (slightly less stable) |
| A. Refit | Online Retail II | +0.001 [−0.007, +0.011] |
| B. Unchanged-behaviour subset | Dunnhumby | −0.008 ns |
| B. Unchanged-behaviour subset | Online Retail II | +0.015 † |

- **Interpretation.** At matched concept count the temporal gap is no longer significant on either dataset. Fuzzy retains about 92 vs 55 concepts on Dunnhumby and 77 vs 49 on Retail II. Non-significance at matched count is not a demonstration of equal stability.
- **Caveat.** Truncation keeps fuzzy's highest-support concepts, so the diagnostic supports a "concept count and/or concept size" explanation, not concept count alone as the cause. In no case is there evidence that fuzzification makes segments *more* stable overall.

## Kuznetsov-FCA (excluded component; ablation evidence)

- **Predictive.** No consistent benefit over fuzzy RFM-FCA. The only significant pooled difference is +0.003 AUC on Retail II, and it is positive at just 1 of 5 origins. All other metrics show no significant difference.
- **Structural.** It is the least stable arm on every measure:
  - refit core Jaccard is 0.14 below crisp on Dunnhumby and 0.07 below on Retail II;
  - only 64% (Dunnhumby) and 81% (Retail II) of its concepts recur across refits;
  - it is less stable in both quarterly re-segmentation subsets.
- **Mechanism.** Its threshold (loss ≤ 5.4e-20) is below float64 resolution and acts as an absolute customer-count rule that changes with training-set size.

It is excluded from the final method; all its code and results are preserved.

## Base-paper reconstruction (descriptive)

Our reconstruction of Rungruang et al. (2024) on Online Retail II recovers all 31 published frequent concept intents. Only 3 of 31 customer counts match exactly, consistent with the paper's unspecified tie-breaking among the 1,623 customers with F = 1 (`results/fair_comparison_retail2/published_table7_reconstruction.csv`; `docs/base_paper_methodology_audit.md`).
