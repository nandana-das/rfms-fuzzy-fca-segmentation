### Figure 1
**Figure 1. Empirical comparison of Crisp RFM-FCA and Baseline Fuzzy RFM-FCA across rolling-origin evaluation windows.**  
Performance is evaluated across two longitudinal retail transaction datasets: Dunnhumby ($K=4$ rolling origins) and Online Retail II ($K=5$ rolling origins). Panels display downstream predictive utility across three distinct evaluation tasks: **(a)** Repurchase ROC AUC in a 90-day holdout horizon; **(b)** Future Spend $R^2$; and **(c)** Future Invoice-count $R^2$. Colored bars denote the unweighted arithmetic mean across evaluation origins for Crisp RFM-FCA (amber) and Baseline Fuzzy RFM-FCA ($M_0$, teal). Overlaid white circular markers connected by dotted lines depict paired performance for each specific rolling-origin evaluation cohort. Horizontal brackets above bar pairs indicate the mean performance delta ($\Delta = \bar{M}_{\text{Fuzzy}} - \bar{M}_{\text{Crisp}}$) along with the fraction of origins in which Fuzzy RFM-FCA strictly outperforms Crisp RFM-FCA. Across all nine evaluation origins in both datasets, Fuzzy RFM-FCA achieves uniform positive improvements across all three predictive metrics.

### Figure 2
**Figure 2. Evaluation of Tail-Sensitive Membership Functions ($M_0$ vs. $M_1$) across rolling evaluation origins.**  
Comparison of Baseline Fuzzy RFM-FCA ($M_0$, standard Zadeh triangular-trapezoidal membership functions, teal) against Tail-Sensitive Fuzzy RFM-FCA ($M_1$, log-additive Pareto piecewise membership functions, vermilion) across Dunnhumby ($K=4$) and Online Retail II ($K=5$). Panels display: **(a)** Repurchase ROC AUC; **(b)** Future Spend $R^2$; and **(c)** Future Invoice-count $R^2$. Axis ranges, tick spacing, and geometric proportions are standardized to match Figure 1 for direct cross-figure comparability. Bars represent unweighted origin means, and overlaid markers indicate origin-specific evaluation pairs. Brackets show mean difference ($\Delta = \bar{M}_{M1} - \bar{M}_{M0}$) and positive origin ratios. On Dunnhumby, $M_1$ yields modest, consistent improvements across all metrics and origins. On Online Retail II, $M_1$ delivers a substantial, uniform improvement in invoice-count prediction ($\Delta = +0.028$, $5/5$ origins), while repeat-purchase discrimination gains are cohort-dependent (positive in $3/5$ origins).

### Figure 3
**Figure 3. Paired per-origin metric differences ($\Delta = M_1 - M_0$) across longitudinal evaluation cohorts.**  
Origin-by-origin performance deltas for Tail-Sensitive Fuzzy RFM-FCA ($M_1$) relative to baseline ($M_0$) across Dunnhumby (top row: panels a–c) and Online Retail II (bottom row: panels d–f). Panels depict: **(a, d)** $\Delta$ Repurchase ROC AUC; **(b, e)** $\Delta$ Future Spend $R^2$; and **(c, f)** $\Delta$ Future Invoice-count $R^2$. Each vertical stem (lollipop) indicates the exact paired difference for a specific rolling-origin date or day index; vermilion markers denote performance gains ($M_1 > M_0$), while blue markers denote performance degradations ($M_1 < M_0$). The solid black horizontal line represents the zero-reference threshold ($\Delta = 0$), and the dashed gray horizontal line indicates the cross-origin mean difference. Dashed connecting lines trace cohort-to-cohort trajectory dynamics. Corner badges summarize the mean delta and origin win counts. The figure highlights that while Future Spend $R^2$ and Future Invoice-count $R^2$ exhibit consistent positive shifts across all cohorts, the aggregate ROC AUC gain on Online Retail II is dominated by the initial origin (`2010-09-10`, $+0.0180$), with subsequent cohorts fluctuating symmetrically around zero (median difference $-0.00002$).

### Figure 4
**Figure 4. Segmentation stability evaluation under random data perturbation and quarterly longitudinal refitting.**  
Comparison of concept stability between Crisp RFM-FCA (amber) and Baseline Fuzzy RFM-FCA ($M_0$, teal). **(a)** Subsampling stability on Dunnhumby across 50 iterations of 80% random customer subsampling relative to the full-cohort lattice, evaluated via Core Jaccard ($\mu \ge 0.5$), Fuzzy Jaccard (graded membership similarity), and Concept Recurrence. **(b)** Subsampling stability on Online Retail II under the identical 80% resampling protocol. **(c)** Longitudinal re-segmentation stability measured via temporal Core Jaccard across consecutive quarterly lattices for All Customers (unfiltered cohort) and Stable Customers (customers actively purchasing in both quarters). Values above bars indicate metric heights; brackets denote difference ($\Delta = \text{Fuzzy} - \text{Crisp}$). Under 80% subsampling, both methods achieve high core concept stability ($>0.95$), though Crisp RFM-FCA displays slightly higher Fuzzy Jaccard and Concept Recurrence across both datasets. In longitudinal quarterly re-segmentation across the entire customer base, Crisp RFM-FCA is significantly more stable ($p = 0.004$ on both datasets), whereas Fuzzy RFM-FCA demonstrates equivalent or slightly higher stability only among persistent, stable customers on Online Retail II. These findings confirm that Fuzzy RFM-FCA's empirical superiority is localized to predictive expressiveness rather than partition rigidity.
